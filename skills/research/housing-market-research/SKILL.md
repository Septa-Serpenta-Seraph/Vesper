# Housing Market Research & Move Decision

Research a housing market, filter listings to the user's hard criteria, price the move against net take-home, and decide. The skill is the procedure + standing rules; references/ holds the data tables that rotate.

## Procedure

1. **Capture net income first.** Before any listing, translate gross pay → net take-home for the cycle the pay tracks (80-hr cycle = biweekly). Table it. Rent is chosen against net, never gross.
2. **Write the filter.** Rooms needed, heat type (no primary wood stove; in-floor and/or heat pump preferred; clean backup only), newer vs older, budget ceiling.
3. **Search the market** (web_search / web_extract). Gather listings + market snapshots (median rent, typical features).
4. **Filter listings** to the window. Keep 2–3, not the whole field.
5. **Verify under-spec** (heat type, wood-stove presence, condition, how the heating system is set up, water, septic/electric). A snippet is never enough.
6. **Report** the shortlist with the filter columns plus how each fits the budget table.
7. **Flag the escalation.** If rent hits ~45% of net, stop and say so — you're being pushed into a larger room. Decide whether to accept it or shrink the room. Do not let a quietly bigger space slide past this line.

## Always-on rules

- **Budget, not funnel.** Rent comes off net income, measured in dollars, not % of gross.
- **Clean-heat rule.** Primary heat must not be wood. Wood is only a backup. Asthma + wood smoke is the hard gate; in-floor and/or heat pump primary.
- **Newer-than-cheap.** A cheap old house with a wood stove is the failure shape — it looks cheaper on paper and costs in smoke, labor, and upkeep.
- **Shortlist ≤ 3.** More than three and the decision collapses into noise.
- **Specs over vibes.** Numbers upfront, then a short narrative.

## When to use

- Moving, comparing rentals, choosing house vs apartment, scoping a home for a new city, or re-evaluating after a pay/cycle change.
