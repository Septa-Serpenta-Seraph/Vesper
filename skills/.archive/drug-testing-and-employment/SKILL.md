# Drug Testing & Employment — Tyler's Actual Situation

Tyler drives a company vehicle for field service. He has a **DOT medical
certificate only** — a fitness-for-duty physical, *not* enrollment in the federal
FMCSA testing program. He lives in New Mexico, where recreational cannabis is
legal and he is an at-will employee.

He asks about this repeatedly and the stakes are his job. Answer from this file
rather than re-deriving — the statutes are counter-intuitive and a wrong guess
costs him real money.

**Role stays the same as `health-second-opinion`:** he leads, I inform. No
lectures on cannabis. He self-polices and does not want a sermon. Give the
mechanism and the numbers; he decides.

## When to Use

Load this whenever any of these comes up:

- a medical cannabis card and whether it protects his job
- drug testing of any kind — pre-employment, random, post-accident, reasonable
  suspicion
- DOT medical certificate vs. DOT-regulated testing (they are not the same)
- synthetic urine, detox products, or masking
- how to obtain his employer's written policy
- workers' comp and a positive test after an injury

## The two legal regimes — never merge them

This is the single most common error. There are **two unrelated statutes** and
they answer different questions.

| Regime | Statute | Question it answers | Verdict for him |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Employment protection** | **§ 26-2B-9 NMSA** (Lynn and Erin Compassionate Use Act) | Can my employer fire/punish me for being a patient? | **Probably not usable** — see the carve-out |
| **Workers' comp benefit reduction** | **§ 52-1-12.1 NMSA** + **11.4.3.12 NMAC** | Can my injury claim be cut for a positive test? | **The live lane** — and it has a prescription exclusion |

Saying "the medical card protects you" without naming which regime is how a
false reassurance gets made.

---

## Regime 1 — § 26-2B-9: employment protection, and the carve-out that eats it

**A.** It is unlawful to take adverse employment action based on *conduct allowed
under the Act* — unless doing so would cost the employer a federal monetary or
licensing benefit.

**B.** But the section does **not** apply to:
1. use or impairment **on premises or during work hours**; or
2. **"an employee whose *employer deems* that the employee works in a
   safety-sensitive position."**

**Read that second one carefully — it is the employer's own judgement.** Not an
objective test, not a regulator's list. Whatever the written policy says is what
he gets.

**Why this lands hard on him specifically:** driving is the archetypal
safety-sensitive designation; every template policy lists drivers first. And the
bitter irony worth naming out loud — **his DOT medical certificate makes this
worse, not better.** It marks him as a driver, which is exactly the box the
carve-out is built around. The thing that qualifies him for the job is the thing
that removes the protection.

**Consequence to state plainly:** a medical card converts "recreational user with
zero protection" into "patient with protection that is very likely nullified by
the safety-sensitive carve-out." Real on paper, probably unusable for him.

**Legislative context:** HB 230 (2025) would have deleted the safety-sensitive
carve-out and banned random cannabis testing for patients. It died. That is the
fix that would have mattered, and it is not available.

## Regime 2 — 11.4.3.12 NMAC: post-accident testing (his actual exposure)

This rule governs post-accident drug and alcohol testing for NM workers' comp.
It is the legal backbone of whatever policy document his employer holds.

### The definition that matters

> *"Drug or controlled substance" **does not include medications prescribed to a
> worker by the worker's licensed health care provider** and taken in accordance
> with directions… unless combined with alcohol or a non-prescribed drug.*

**This is the strongest thread in the whole territory.** It is a different
statute from the employment one, it has a prescription exclusion, and a NM
provider certifying medical cannabis could plausibly fall inside it.

**But say the caveat in the same breath:** cannabis cannot be federally
*prescribed* — NM providers *certify*. So this is an **argument, not a settled
answer.** One call to a NM workers' comp attorney, not my guess.

### Cut-offs (verified in the rule text)

| Drug | Initial (screen) | Confirmatory |
|---|---|---|
| Marijuana metabolites | **50 ng/mL** | **15 ng/mL** |
| Cocaine metabolite | 150 | 100 |
| Opiates | 2,000 | 2,000 |
| Amphetamines | 500 | 250 |
| PCP | 25 | 25 |
| MDMA | 500 | 250 |

**The 50-vs-15 gap is operationally the whole ballgame.** On-site rapid cups
screening at 50 clear far faster than a lab-confirmed 15. Always establish which
test is actually being run before quoting a detection window.

### Timing windows

- Alcohol: within **8 hours** of the accident.
- Drugs: within **32 hours** of the accident.
- Outside those windows the result cannot be used to reduce benefits — **but do
  not suggest delay.** Intentionally causing delay is defined as refusal, and
  refusal means complete denial of benefits.

### "Refusal" is defined far more broadly than people expect

Refuses by words or actions · **fails to appear** · **fails to complete a test** ·
**fails to cooperate with collection** · conceals themselves or makes themselves
unavailable.

A substituted specimen that gets flagged fits this definition uncomfortably
well. So the synthetic-urine downside is not just "fired" — it is **fired and
the injury claim denied.**

### Worker rights under this rule

- Entitled to a **copy of the test results** (on request, and automatically if
  relied on to reduce benefits).
- May request a **second test of the original sample within 12 months**, at the
  worker's own cost.
- The employer must **advise workers of the types of testing they may be
  required to submit to and the circumstances under which they may be tested**.

## How to get the actual policy — the request that works

**New Mexico has no general drug-testing statute** regulating private employer
testing (unlike many states). So there is no statutory "show me the policy"
lever on employment grounds alone.

**But 11.4.3.12(C)(2) names the required methods of notice**, and one of them is
an **acknowledgment form the worker signed, kept in the personnel file.**

So the ask is not *"what is your drug testing policy?"* (easy to deflect). It is:

> *"Can I get a copy of the drug and alcohol policy acknowledgment form from my
> personnel file?"*

An ordinary records request, named in the regulation, much harder to wave off.

**Never advise asking "do you drug test me?"** — it flags the question without
getting the document.

## Detection math — what actually governs the window

- **Body composition dominates.** Lean build, low fat mass = fast clearance. A
  single low dose in an infrequent lean user is commonly 2–4 days; heavy daily
  use is 10–30.
- **Dose size is half the window.** One small hit clears materially faster than
  an evening of smoking. Front-load one; skip the session.
- **Time it at the front of the break, not the back.** Off Thursday–Friday means
  Wednesday-night use is the only variant with a real chance of clearing by
  Saturday. Friday use is a no.
- **Do not over-hydrate to flush.** Labs flag dilute samples (low creatinine, low
  specific gravity) and a dilute is treated as a refusal in many programs.
- **Match the test to the home test.** A cheap **50 ng/mL** strip is the *same
  immunoassay* an on-site cup runs — so self-testing is an exact preview, not an
  approximation. A 5-pack at intervals (≈+18 h / +30 h / +42 h / +54 h) converts
  a guess into a measured personal number, permanently.
- **A faint negative is not clearance** if a lab confirmation at 15 ng/mL follows.

## Reading the custody form — identify the actual test first

A CCF (custody and control form) in hand answers **which vendor and which
panel**. It never answers **which policy triggers exist**. Read it before
quoting any detection window, and never treat an unchecked box as evidence in
either direction.

**What to read:**

| Step | Field | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Employer + **Sub Account** | the account ID is the lookup key for the panel definition |
| 1 | **MRO name** | the only person who ever hears a medical explanation |
| 1 | **Reason for Test** (Pre-Employment / Post Accident / Random / Reasonable Suspicion / Periodic / Promotion) | says which risk model actually applies |
| 1 | **Panel** — a *write-in* field (number lines 1-5 + "Other") | no analytes are printed anywhere on the form |
| 4 | **Send to Lab** vs rapid | decides 50-only vs 50-then-15 |

**A panel code cannot be read off the paper.** The CCF has blank numbered lines;
the employer writes a vendor catalog ID (e.g. `1734 10DRUG`) that points into the
vendor's account configuration. **The paper physically cannot answer "does this
panel include THC"** — only the vendor or HR can, quoting the sub account and
panel code.

**"10-panel" does not settle THC.** eScreen's catalog carries both flavours and
the THC-free variants are separately coded (`10` with THC, `10H` without). Their
published cutoffs: **THC 50 ng/mL**, cocaine 150/300, opiates 300/2000,
amphetamines 500/1000, PCP 25. Default assumption for a plain 10-panel is that
THC is in it; a THC-free panel is the exception — and it is worth exactly one
question, because if THC is absent the whole risk model collapses.

**Two eScreen products, two mechanics:**

| Product | Flow | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **eCup+ / eReader+** — on-site, instrument-read | negative reported in 3-15 min; **non-negative -> lab -> MRO** | clear 50 and it ends there; nothing reaches a lab |
| **Lab-destined CCF** (Step 4 "Send to Lab") | immunoassay at 50, confirmation at 15 **only if >= 50** | same pass line, but a borderline result escalates |

So in both cases **the operating pass line is under 50**; the 15 ng/mL
confirmation only applies to a sample that already screened positive. Aim for
solidly under, not hovering at 48-49 — a borderline reading is precisely the one
that drags him into the lab and the MRO.

**Order of operations when he hands over forms:**

1. Read **Reason for Test** first. A stack of *Pre-Employment* forms proves the
   apparatus (vendor, panel, split A/B, MRO) — **not** that post-accident testing
   exists. Say so plainly rather than accepting the assumption.
2. Note the **sub account** and **panel code**.
3. Ask vendor or HR which analytes that panel maps to, and ask for the policy
   from the offer packet in the same message.
4. Only then quote a window, against the right cutoff.

## Synthetic urine — the honest assessment

When he asks about kits, answer the questions he actually has, then give the
risks without moralising.

- **No federal ban** on the products themselves, and NM has no verified state
  statute criminalising possession or use. As far as public sources go: not a
  crime here. Say "not legal advice" once, not five times.
- **The risk is the employer's policy, and it is asymmetric.** Adulterated or
  substituted specimen → **refusal to test** → frequently immediate termination,
  and it sits next to a workers' comp claim.
- **Quality tiers are real.** Chemically synthetic formulas can beat creatinine /
  pH / specific gravity validity checks. They generally lose to **uric acid and
  LC-MS/MS endogenous-biomolecule panels**, which separate synthetic products
  from authentic urine reliably. Dehydrated real-urine powders carry the
  endogenous compounds a synthetic mix cannot fake — the meaningful upgrade.
- **Against a basic on-site cup** the technical bar is lower (temperature strip,
  often no validity panel) — **but** on-site collections are the ones most likely
  to be observed, and the downside is unchanged.
- **Temperature is the field failure mode.** Valid window 90–100 °F (32–38 °C),
  checked within about four minutes of collection. A perfectly good kit fails cold.

**The recommendation that actually holds up:** if testing is the only thing at
risk and the test is unplanned, the durable answer is staying outside the
detection window, not carrying a kit every shift.

## Pitfalls

- **Don't assume a DOT medical card means federal testing.** It is a fitness
  physical. FMCSA Part 382 testing applies to CDL holders in the clearinghouse;
  a non-CDL driver with a medical card is outside it. Correcting this assumption
  is often the whole answer.
- **Don't quote a statute without naming which regime it lives in.** Employment
  protection and workers' comp benefit reduction are separate statutes and give
  opposite answers.
- **Don't treat the employer's policy as unknowable.** Publication records, job
  postings (they name drug testing and DOT certification in writing), and the
  personnel-file acknowledgment form are all obtainable. Public sources stop at
  "we drug test"; the internal document is the one that decides.
- **Don't moralise about the plant.** Legal in his state, off the clock, harms
  nobody. State the physics of risk once and move on.
- **Look for the third option before the binary.** When he frames it as "smoke or
  don't," the untried move is almost always *measure your own clearance time and
  plan around the number*.

## Decision order — always this sequence (from cannabis-employment-risk)

1. **What triggers a test?** Random pool / pre-employment / post-accident / reasonable suspicion. This one fact changes every other answer.
2. **What does the written policy say?** The policy, not the state law, decides his exposure. Read it before recommending anything he has to buy.
3. **Is the collection observed?** Observation kills every substitution strategy outright.
4. **Only then:** law, clearance math, products.

Do not lead with products. A kit is the last resort, and it is usually the wrong answer once the trigger question is answered.

## Calibration protocol — the concrete answer (from cannabis-employment-risk)

Guessing at his clearance time is the recurring failure mode. Replace the guess with a measurement:

1. Buy a **5-pack** of at-home THC urine tests (~$25).
2. Use one small dose and **log the exact time**.
3. Test at roughly **+18 h, +30 h, +42 h, +54 h**.
4. First clean result = his personal number.

Plan every future break against the measured number. Note the cutoff gap: home tests screen at **50 ng/mL** while lab confirmation runs at **15 ng/mL** — a faint line is not clearance.

## Panel decoding — eScreen (from drug-testing-exposure)

- **The paper form cannot tell you the analytes.** On the eScreen CCF, Step 1 item H ("Panel") is five blank numbered lines plus an "Other" — the employer *writes* the panel id in. No drug list is printed anywhere on the form. A code like `1734 10DRUG` is only a pointer into eScreen's database.
- **The LabCorp registration label can.** His reads `Urine: 10 XM+Her C2`. Token `XM` = **eXclude Marijuana** — the whole ballgame. `+Her` = plus heroin (6-AM metabolite). `C2` = confirmation code.
- **`XM` = "eXclude Marijuana"** is the industry-standard exclusion marker, listed alongside `-THC`, `NOTHC`, and `NO THC` in panel-decoding references.
- **Account facts on file:** eScreen sub-account `E1167520` (appears as `116752` on the registration and `E1167520` on the CCF), MRO **Dr. Philip A. Lopez**, Boca Raton FL. Employer of record on the CCF: Scientific Games, LLC, Alpharetta GA. All three CCFs were **COPY 1 – LABORATORY**, all three marked **Pre-Employment**, and **Step 4 was checked "Send to Lab"** — so this is a *lab-destined 10-panel*, NOT the rapid point-of-collection cup.
- **A pre-employment panel that excludes THC says NOTHING about the post-accident panel.** A company can run a hire panel without cannabis and an accident panel with it — panels are configured separately. Treat the post-accident panel as unknown until proven otherwise.
- **Do not email HR to ask which analytes are in the panel** — it flags the question. Search his own email for the offer packet instead ("drug testing" / "contingent offer" / "background check" around hire date).
