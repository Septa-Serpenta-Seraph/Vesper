## Interaction rules learned

- **No oral decongestants with a PDE5 inhibitor.** Pseudoephedrine raises BP
  and directly fights the drug. This is the one real hazard in the sinus story.
- **Omeprazole** is safe alongside both tadalafil and trazodone (no meaningful
  CYP clash) — but it's a *morning* drug, 30–60 min before food. For night
  heartburn the right tool is **famotidine (Pepcid)**, PRN. Long-term PPI →
  B12 and magnesium depletion, reduced calcium absorption.
- **"B vitamins give you energy" is a myth.** They're enzyme cofactors, not
  stimulants; they only "give energy" by correcting a deficiency, over weeks.
  The doses in his blend are near-RDA, not megadoses. Only B12 at 1000 mcg has
  a scattered anecdotal *activating* report — if he's wired, that's the suspect.
  The magnesium in it is sleep-friendly (oxide is the poorly absorbed form; may
  loosen stools — that's the likelier effect).
- **Alcohol amplifies the BP drop.** Physics, stated once, no sermon.
- **Grapefruit juice** is a CYP3A4 inhibitor — spikes tadalafil levels.

## Risk numbers on file (never drop these unscaled again)

- PDE5i effect on seated BP, daily dosing: **~1–3 mmHg**. Less than standing
  up, a hot shower, or a heavy meal.
- Symptomatic dizziness in trials: **~1–2%** vs ~1% on placebo → **~95%
  chance he feels nothing**; ~90–95% with trazodone stacked.
- **NAION:** ~**2.8 per 100,000 patient-years** on a PDE5i vs **2.5–11.8 per
  100,000/yr** baseline in men 50+ taking nothing — i.e. inside the baseline
  range. The original scare came from naming it with no number attached.
- Things that push the dizziness odds down: alcohol, dehydration, a hot shower
  right after dosing, standing fast from lying down.
