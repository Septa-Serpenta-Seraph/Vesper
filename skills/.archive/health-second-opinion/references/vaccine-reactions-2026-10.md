# Vaccine reactions — the local-node pattern (2026-10-10 / 10-11)

Tyler gets a COVID/flu shot on a Friday, then brings me an unexplained body
sensation a couple of days later. This is the shape to recognise instantly,
because it is benign, common, and *looks alarming* when you don't know the
pattern.

---

## The case

**Friday 10/9:** COVID + flu vaccination.

**Sunday 10/11, 5:40 PM:** *"I've had this pain in my muscle just under my left
armpit and idk where it came from."*

**Sunday 10/11, 10:38 PM:** still there; the shot site itself still sore; and —
the part that worried him — *"I've never had soreness in the muscle in my armpit
closest to my chest."*

**Monday 10/12:** settled, no escalation.

---

## The diagnosis, in one move

**Ask which arm the shot went into.** It was the **left**. Left arm shot →
left armpit pain, ~2.5 days later.

**That is the whole answer: vaccine-associated axillary lymphadenopathy.**

- The **axillary lymph nodes drain the upper arm** — including the deltoid, the
  injection site.
- A working immune response makes them swell.
- A swollen node in the axilla sits *deep, pressed against the underside of the
  pectoralis major* — so it presents as **a sore muscle under the armpit that
  you can't place**. That is why the complaint is phrased as muscle pain.

**The anatomy sentence that closes it:** *"The muscle in your armpit closest to
your chest is the pectoralis major — the front wall of the axilla. And that's
exactly where the lymph nodes sit."* It stops being a mystery symptom and
becomes the same event wearing a different face.

## Why "I've never had this before" is expected, not alarming

He'd never had a shot produce this much nodal swelling, so he'd never had a
reason to *notice* that spot. **Reframe it that way:** he isn't discovering
something wrong, he's feeling a place he's never had cause to feel. That single
reframe does most of the reassurance work — far more than "it's probably fine."

⚠️ **Do not dismiss the novelty signal.** He flags "I've never had this" because
it matters. Receive it, then explain *why* it's novel without being wrong.

## The timeline (for expectation-setting)

| Phase | When |
|---|---|
| Nodal swelling appears | **2–4 days** post-shot ← his fit this pattern exactly |
| Shot-site soreness | 3–5 days, entirely normal |
| Nodal swelling resolves | **~1–2 weeks**, usually under a week |

**Nothing to treat.** Cool compress if it's uncomfortable; NSAIDs if genuinely
sore (interaction-checked — ibuprofen is fine with his tadalafil/trazodone
stack, taken with food and water). **Do not massage it** — that's the one thing
that actually aggravates it. Keep using the arm gently; don't let it stiffen.

## The practical nugget worth repeating every time

**Tell any imaging tech about a recent vaccine.** Swollen axillary nodes get
picked up as incidental findings on chest/axillary scans, and there's a whole
literature of people sent down unnecessary workups because nobody knew about the
shot. Say it up front and it's a non-event. Especially relevant for him given
family imaging history — he'll have scans eventually.

## When a red flag is REQUIRED — and how to scale it

Left-sided chest/armpit pain means the cardiac flags get **named out loud**, not
skipped. But they are named *as the exception*, in one compressed block, and
then explicitly dismissed for the current picture:

> *"The four things that turn this into an ER tonight rather than a 'huh, that's
> odd': shortness of breath · chest pressure or pain spreading to your arm, jaw
> or back · sweating, nausea or palpitations with it · sudden severe pain with
> breathlessness. Left-sided, so I'm saying those out loud rather than skipping
> them. If none of that's happening — and nothing you've described suggests it
> is — then it's sleep and report in the morning."*

Then the **check-in-the-morning** criteria, which are the local-infection set:
- Fever
- Redness or heat spreading across the skin
- The area going hard and hot
- A lump growing rather than shrinking

Any → get seen. None → the reaction doing its job.

This keeps the bare-warning rule from SKILL.md: the flags arrive with their
scaling attached, not as a naked threat list.

## Differential — what else it could have been, and why it wasn't

Ranked as I actually offered them, before the arm question resolved it:

1. **Vaccine axillary lymphadenopathy** — ipsilateral, 2–4 days. *Fits exactly.*
2. **Tadalafil myalgia** — a known side effect, usually back / legs / arms, and
   day 7 of daily dosing is when steady state lands. Kept as a live candidate
   until the side resolved it. If it had moved around or appeared elsewhere,
   this becomes the primary.
3. **Plain strain** — long drive Friday, long workday, keyboard posture. The
   muscles of the armpit fold take that load.

Discriminating questions worth asking: **is there a lump** (node) vs **no lump,
just sore muscle**; **does it hurt with movement / deep breath / constantly**
(muscle / rib / nerve-or-node); **any rash, tingling or burning on the skin**
(early shingles, which starts as pain before anything shows).

## The generalisable pattern

**A new symptom arriving 1–4 days after a vaccination, in the right place, has a
mechanical explanation that is boring and expected.** Ask *which arm* first —
it's one question, it's free, and it collapses the search space the same way
"where is the command block" does in the Minecraft skill.

Same family as the SKILL.md rule about a third source arriving mid-stream: when
he does something to himself mid-episode, **rule it in without reopening the
original diagnosis.**
