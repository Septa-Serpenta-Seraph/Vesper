# Testing methods, validity panels, and kits

Absorbed from `cannabis-employment-risk` (references/testing-methods-and-kits.md).

## Specimen types and windows

- **Urine** — the default for employer programs. THC-COOH detection window is the long one; this is the model to reason from unless told otherwise.
- **Oral fluid** — roughly 24–48 h. Far shorter than urine; a different answer entirely.
- **Hair** — expensive and rare in employer programs. If he says they will not pay for it, believe him and move on.
- **Blood** — hours to ~2 days; used for post-accident and impairment work.

## Specimen validity testing (SVT)

**Standard panel: creatinine, pH, specific gravity.** This is exactly what commercial kits are engineered to pass.

**Advanced tier: uric acid and LC-MS/MS endogenous-biomolecule panels.** In a *Journal of Analytical Toxicology* comparison of eight synthetic products against 43 known-authentic specimens, uric acid and LC-MS/MS each separated the two groups correctly, while a commercial spot test performed less consistently.

**Consequence:** a synthetic formula is a chemical approximation and fails the advanced tier. **Dehydrated real urine** is the only category that starts from biological material and therefore carries the endogenous compounds.

## Temperature is the field failure mode

Valid submission window: **90–100 °F (32–38 °C)**, checked within roughly four minutes of collection. Cold is the most common way a good kit fails. A body-worn reservoir solves this passively; heat pads are a race against the clock, and a twelve-hour shift in a hot vehicle is a losing version of it.

## Statutory detail — New Mexico

- **§ 26-2B-9(A)** — unlawful to take adverse employment action against an applicant or employee based on conduct allowed under the Lynn and Erin Compassionate Use Act, *unless* doing so would cause the employer to lose a monetary or licensing-related federal benefit.
- **§ 26-2B-9(B)(1)** — no protection for use of, or impairment by, medical cannabis on the premises or during the hours of employment.
- **§ 26-2B-9(B)(2)** — the section does **not apply** to "an employee whose employer deems that the employee works in a safety-sensitive position."
- **Medical patients are protected; recreational users are unprotected at-will employees.**
- **HB 230** — would have deleted the safety-sensitive carve-out and barred random cannabis testing for patients. It died in the Senate. Cite it as the fix that has not passed, never as pending law.

## Synthetic urine and the law

No federal ban on the products themselves. Roughly 29 states have statutes covering synthetic urine, masking products, or substances/devices intended to defraud a test — and several reach **possession and use**, not just sale, with penalties from infraction to felony. **New Mexico is not among the states verified with such a statute**, but this changes; re-verify against the official state code before relying on it.

**Federally:** under DOT rules a verified adulterated or substituted specimen is handled as a **refusal to test**.

## Adulteration hazard

"Natural male enhancement" products and grey-market "detox" blends are routinely spiked with actual PDE5 inhibitors. Anyone already on a prescription PDE5 inhibitor who takes one has taken an accidental double dose. Roughly two-thirds of ingredients in popular ED supplements have no solid RCT backing — the label is not evidence of contents.
