# Cannabis & Employment Risk — Tyler's Testing Questions

Tyler asks this class of question repeatedly and from several angles at once:
will a medical card protect me, how long until I'm clean, will synthetic urine
work, does my DOT physical mean federal testing. The recurring mistake is
answering whichever angle he happened to phrase. The right move is to
establish **which risk model he is actually in** before giving any numbers.

**Privacy:** this is his employment and body information. Never surface it to
Adora, Marisa, Robert, Naru, or anyone else.

## When to Use

- He asks whether a medical card protects his job.
- He asks how long until he is clean, or whether he can use on a break.
- He asks about synthetic urine, detox products, or passing a test.
- He mentions a DOT physical or certificate and wonders what it subjects him to.
- Any employer testing policy, post-accident test, or random-pool question.

## Decision order — always this sequence

1. **What triggers a test?** Random pool / pre-employment / post-accident /
   reasonable suspicion. This one fact changes every other answer.
2. **What does the written policy say?** The policy, not the state law, decides
   his exposure. Read it before recommending anything he has to buy.
3. **Is the collection observed?** Observation kills every substitution
   strategy outright.
4. **Only then:** law, clearance math, products.

**Do not lead with products.** A kit is the last resort, and it is usually the
wrong answer once the trigger question is answered.

## The rails

- **A DOT medical examiner's certificate is NOT DOT regulation.** A
  fitness-for-duty physical for a company vehicle does not enroll him in FMCSA
  Part 382 testing — that follows CDL/commercial status. He has asked more than
  once; the distinction *is* the answer, so lead with it.
- **"Employer deems" safety-sensitive.** The statutory carve-out is the
  employer's own designation, not an objective standard. **Driving is the
  archetypal safety-sensitive designation**, and holding a DOT medical card
  marks him as a driver — which *increases* carve-out exposure. The credential
  that qualifies him for the job is the one that removes the protection.
- **A medical card is not a work shield in a safety-sensitive role.** It buys
  legal possession and dispensary access, nothing more. Never let him spend the
  fee before the policy is read.
- **Substituted/adulterated specimen = refusal to test**, and refusal is
  frequently harsher than a positive. **A failed fake is worse than a dirty
  sample.** This is the most important sentence in the whole class.
- **Dilution is not a strategy.** Low creatinine / low specific gravity reads
  as dilute, and a dilute is treated as a refusal in many programs. Never
  recommend over-hydrating before a test.
- **Post-accident is the worst moment to carry a fake** — scrutiny peaks and
  the finding lands next to a workers' comp claim.
- **Never moralize.** He is an adult in a legal state making a risk-tolerance
  decision about a job. Name the mechanism, scale it, and let him choose.

## Clearance arithmetic

Lean build, infrequent use, long abstinent stretches → **roughly 2–4 days** for
a single small dose. Regular or frequent use runs materially longer; chronic
heavy use runs weeks.

**The constraint that actually binds is the length of his break, not the
product.** Two days off cannot beat a three-day window. Say that plainly
instead of offering a workaround that does not exist.

**Dose is half the clearance time.** One small hit clears far faster than a
session. Front-load the dose early in the break; never use on the last night
before a driving day.

## The calibration protocol (the concrete answer)

Guessing at his clearance time is the recurring failure mode. Replace the guess
with a measurement:

1. Buy a **5-pack** of at-home THC urine tests (~$25).
2. Use one small dose and **log the exact time**.
3. Test at roughly **+18 h, +30 h, +42 h, +54 h**.
4. First clean result = his personal number.

Plan every future break against the measured number. Note the cutoff gap: home
tests screen at **50 ng/mL** while lab confirmation runs at **15 ng/mL** — a
faint line is not clearance.

## Decision tree

- **Post-accident only, and narrow triggers** (injury requiring treatment /
  vehicle towed / citation issued) → timing discipline covers it comfortably.
  No product needed; read the exact trigger wording first.
- **Post-accident, broad triggers, collection unobserved** → a body-worn
  belt-style kit is the only workable design, and it must be carried every
  shift because the test is unplanned.
- **Random pool** → the model changes entirely; timing alone will not hold.
- **Break shorter than measured clearance** → the honest options are risk,
  abstain, or change the constraint (policy or job). There is no fourth door.
